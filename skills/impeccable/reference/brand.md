# Brand surfaces

Use for landing pages, campaigns, portfolios, and content-led sites whose visual identity helps communicate the subject. This reference supplies design criteria, not additional approval gates.

## Direction

Ground the design in real content, audience, and the desired action or impression. Use existing identity and approved references. For a new direction, describe the composition, tone, and visual choices that make it fit this particular brief. Familiarity and distinctiveness are both useful; neither novelty nor restraint is a universal goal.

A category is context, not a recipe. A restaurant can be spare or expressive; a technical brand can use a serif or a system font. Judge the actual result rather than rejecting a named style because it is common.

## Typography

Inspect the project's existing fonts and licensed local assets first. Choose for legibility, language coverage, tone, and the shapes of the real headings. A single family with clear weight/size contrast can carry the page; add a family when it has a distinct role.

Tune type scale, measure, line-height, and tracking together. Fluid headings can help across viewports; inspect long words and translated content. Use catalog/database research only when it resolves an actual choice, and keep public font services out of self-hosted runtime pages.

## Color

Choose semantic palette roles before individual values. Useful strategies include restrained neutrals with an accent, a prominent brand color, or a richer palette with distinct roles. Select the strategy from the subject, content, context, and existing identity. Warm neutrals, monochrome, saturated fields, and light/dark themes are all valid when they serve the brief.

Verify text and control contrast on the actual backgrounds. Preserve state semantics and avoid relying on color alone.

## Layout

Use hierarchy, grouping, density, and pacing to guide the reader. Asymmetry, full-bleed sections, cards, and editorial grids are options. Choose repeatable patterns where the content repeats and introduce contrast where it clarifies emphasis. Number sections when sequence matters. Let responsive layout follow the content, not a fixed screenshot.

## Imagery

An approved image-led brief needs its required imagery. Use project assets or licensed sources; verify factual image subjects and URLs. Where appropriate, generated raster assets or code-native scenes can support the direction. Typography-led pages are also valid when the brief supports them.

Do not invent stock photo IDs, testimonials, screenshots, or product imagery. Do not replace an explicitly required image with a colored placeholder and call it complete. If an asset is missing, identify the exact gap and continue independent work. Keep runtime assets local when the user says the target is offline.

Use images purposefully: one strong image can be better than a padded gallery. Write alternate text for its actual informational role; decorative imagery can have empty alt text.

## Motion and review

Use choreography or micro-interactions when they support the tone or explain a transition. Keep navigation and essential content usable immediately, including with reduced motion. Static pages can be finished designs.

Review the real page against the brief: recognizable subject, clear next action, coherent voice, readable hierarchy, usable responsive layout, and complete requested content. Remove decoration when it competes with those aims; preserve expressive choices when they help.
